Custom app or a no-code builder? An honest comparison

We build custom apps, so treat this with the appropriate suspicion. But sending someone to a builder when that is the right answer costs us nothing and saves them a lot, so here is the honest version.

When a builder is the right call

If your app is essentially content in a wrapper, a builder will do it faster and cheaper than we will, and you should use one. A schedule, a directory, a set of articles, a simple loyalty card: these are solved problems.

It is also the right call when you are testing whether anyone wants the thing at all. Spending real money to find out that nobody opens it is the most expensive way to learn a cheap lesson.

And if your budget genuinely does not stretch to a custom build, a builder is a real option and a half-finished custom app is not.

Where builders stop working

The wall is always the same shape: your business does something specific, and the platform has no concept for it.

Maybe the pricing rule depends on who is logged in. Maybe the workflow has an approval step. Maybe the calculation is the entire point of the app. A builder is a set of assumptions about what an app is, and the moment your requirement sits outside those assumptions, there is no option to add it. You either drop the requirement or you leave.

The tell is when you find yourself redesigning your process to fit the tool.

The things people find out late

You do not own it. Stop paying and the app stops existing. That is a subscription to your own product.

Migration is a rebuild. There is no export. Leaving means starting over, which is why the decision feels cheap at the start and expensive later.

Performance has a floor. An app assembled from generic parts feels like one, and users are unforgiving about it in a way they are not about websites.

How to tell which situation you are in

Ask what happens on the app's most important screen. If you can describe it as a list, a form or a page of content, a builder can probably do it.

If describing it requires the word then more than twice, you are describing logic, and logic is where builders end and building begins.

A reasonable middle path

Prove the idea however you can, including on a builder. Then build the real thing once you know what it is.

The mistake is not starting on a builder. The mistake is staying on one after you have outgrown it, because the cost of leaving grows every month you wait.
